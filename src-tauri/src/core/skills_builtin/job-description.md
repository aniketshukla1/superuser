name: job-description
description: Describe a role by what the person will do, so the right people recognise themselves
allowed-tools: write_file
---
Most job descriptions describe a wishlist. The ones that work describe a job.

1. **Start from the work, not the title.** What will this person spend Tuesday doing?
   List three to five real responsibilities with enough specificity that a candidate
   can picture the day. "Own the roadmap" is a title; "decide what ships each month
   with the two engineering leads" is a job.
2. **Say who they work with and who decides.** Reporting line, the people they depend
   on, and what they own outright. Ambiguity here is the single biggest cause of a
   good hire leaving in six months.
3. **Split must-have from nice-to-have honestly**, and keep must-haves under five.
   Every extra requirement narrows the pool, and the evidence is consistent that long
   lists narrow it hardest for candidates who are underrepresented and self-select out.
4. **Write requirements as capabilities, not proxies.** "Can debug a production
   incident end to end" over "5+ years experience" — the proxy excludes people who
   have the capability and includes people who do not.
5. **Say the things people actually decide on**: location and remote policy, salary
   range, level, and how the process runs. A description that hides these wastes the
   time of everyone it does not fit.
6. **Cut the boilerplate.** "Fast-paced environment", "wear many hats", "rockstar" —
   these say nothing, and their presence is itself a signal candidates read.
7. Deliver `outputs/jd-<role>.md`. Flag anything you had to invent — an invented salary
   band or reporting line is worse than a marked gap.

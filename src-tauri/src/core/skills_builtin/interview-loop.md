---
name: interview-loop
description: Design an interview that tests the job, with each signal owned by one stage
allowed-tools: write_file
---
An interview loop fails in two ways: it tests something other than the job, or it
tests the same thing four times and calls it consensus.

1. **List the signals the job actually needs** — from the responsibilities, not from a
   generic template. Four to six. If you cannot say why a signal matters for THIS
   role, it is a tradition rather than a requirement.
2. **Assign each signal to exactly one stage.** Duplication is what makes a loop feel
   thorough and be redundant: four interviewers forming the same impression is one
   data point with four votes.
3. **Design each stage around real work.** A task resembling the job predicts
   performance; a puzzle predicts puzzle-solving. If you use a take-home, timebox it
   honestly and say so — an "hour" that takes six is a filter on free time.
4. **Write the rubric before the interview**, with what a weak, adequate and strong
   answer look like. A rubric written afterwards is a record of who you liked.
5. **Give every candidate the same stages and the same questions.** Improvised loops
   compare candidates against each other rather than the bar, which is where bias does
   its work unnoticed.
6. **Say what would fail someone.** A loop with no failure condition passes whoever
   the panel enjoyed talking to.
7. Deliver `outputs/loop-<role>.md`: the signals, the stage that owns each, the
   questions, the rubric, and what the candidate is told in advance.

name: onboarding-plan
description: Get a new joiner to their first real contribution, with an owner on every step
allowed-tools: table, write_file
---
Most onboarding plans are a list of accounts to create. The measure of a good one is
how soon the person does something real, and whether they knew who to ask.

1. **Name the first real contribution and when it lands.** A shipped fix in week one,
   a customer call taken in week two. Everything else in the plan exists to make that
   possible; anything that does not is filler.
2. **Put an owner and a date on every line.** A plan where "the team" does something is
   a plan where nobody does it. Access requests especially — they are the single
   commonest reason a first week is spent waiting.
3. **Front-load access, and start it before day one.** List every account, repo, group,
   and physical thing, with who grants it and how long each historically takes.
4. **Separate what they must know from what they can look up.** Meetings, docs, and
   recordings expand to fill the first month. Pick the few that change how they work
   and cut the rest to a reading list they can reach for.
5. **Name the people, not the functions.** Who to ask when the build breaks, who owns
   billing, who to sit with in week one. A new joiner's real bottleneck is not knowing
   whose problem something is.
6. **Set checkpoints with a question attached** — end of week one, end of month one,
   end of month three — and say what "on track" looks like at each, so a struggling
   start is noticed by someone other than the person struggling.
7. Deliver `outputs/onboarding-<role>.md` as a table by week: item, owner, done-by.
   Flag anything you assumed about the team's tools or process rather than inventing it.
